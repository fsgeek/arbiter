## The question and why it was the wrong question

The breadcrumb said everything downstream depends on "are the incoherence axes one
or three?" (binding-conflict / granularity / frame-relative). The adversarial pass
found **the binary was a category error.** The answer is **N=2 witnessed orthogonal
axes**; the three named "types" are non-uniform samples of that 2D space, not
points on a line and not three irreducible kinds.

## The two axes that survived adversarial attack on real substrate

**DIM-1 — Observability-locus of the disambiguator.** Where does the fact that
resolves the conflict live? A discrete 3-rung ladder (NOT a continuum):
- outside the prompt, in non-linguistic operating context O — *Type A / Indaleko*
  (file count, index status; commits 24f1885/2f77fce/d6928bb).
- inside the artifact but unnameable — *Type B / case #11* (predictive structure
  in `property_state`; R²_named 0.022–0.035 vs R²_all 0.580–0.961).
- in plain view as a pragmatic frame — *Type C / register* (both fragments
  visible; conflict only in reconciliation; Werdegang).

**DIM-2 — Structural detectability.** Does a syntactic/structural oracle fire from
the artifact alone? {fires | silent}.

## Why they are PROVABLY orthogonal (the load-bearing cross)

The A-vs-C cross witnesses independence directly:
- **Type A:** disambiguator UNOBSERVABLE (outside prompt) yet oracle **FIRES**
  (opposite directives, same referent Record.Attributes).
- **Type C:** disambiguator FULLY OBSERVABLE (both fragments in view) yet oracle
  **SILENT** (returns C=0, fails silently; spine-audit break #3).

Unobservable-yet-detectable and observable-yet-undetectable on the same two points
= two independent axes by construction. This is the **structural re-derivation of
Paper 3's already-proven dissociation** between the 81% within-instruction effect
(0.1567→0.0290, p=0.029) and the r=−0.274 between-instruction topology
(spine-audit break #1). Paper 3 was already measuring two axes and framing it as
one phenomenon.

## What the pass KILLED

- **The δ-as-single-scalar unification (one-axis).** The unifier re-welded 81% and
  r=−0.274 as "executor-blindness depth at two orders" — the exact weld the
  spine-audit broke at break #1, re-attempted by a fresh agent and caught again.
  It self-rated 0.42 (lowest of all six agents) and conceded it needs a per-block
  coupling that was never measured: E-PROC fixed `commit-restrictions`, but
  `commit-restrictions` does not appear in E-TOPO's topology table — the two
  interventions touched DIFFERENT blocks, so the coupling is asserted, never
  measured. **This weld is an attractor**: it is where the problem wants to
  collapse. Worth knowing as a hazard, not just a refutation.
- **"Three irreducible types."** All three separators returned irreducibly_distinct
  (0.68–0.72) BUT each conditioned its verdict on a typing discipline and each, in
  honest_doubt, named the vacuous super-axis ("resolution gap" / "incoherence
  relative to a context") and rejected it on grounds of VACUITY, not impossibility.
  Three independent agents agreeing the unification is *available but empty* is the
  signal: the types are separable but not by a clean third axis tonight.

## The open question only the PI can answer (DIM-1's seam)

In Indaleko the disambiguator lived OUTSIDE the prompt (which dataset is live); the
fix was a governance/operator action (remove a fragment). In case #11 the
disambiguator lives INSIDE the artifact but unnameable (property_state); the fix is
re-graining the constraint. **Were these two different KINDS of fix, or the same
act — "someone outside the executor had to supply what the executor couldn't see"?**
If the same act, DIM-1 is one ordered ladder. If different kinds of repair, DIM-1
is itself two axes and the space is larger than two. The substrate cannot settle
this; PI testimony decides it. (Awaiting Tony's answer as of this writing.)
