## What actually happened this session (verified, committed, signed)

1. **The breadcrumb's open question — "are the incoherence axes one or three?" —
   was a category error.** An adversarial workflow (3 separators, 1 unifier, 1
   dimensional analyst, 1 adjudicator) landed on **N=2 witnessed orthogonal axes**:
   DIM-1 observability-locus of the disambiguator, DIM-2 structural-detectability.
   They are provably orthogonal by the A-vs-C cross (unobservable-yet-detectable
   vs observable-yet-undetectable). This is the structural re-derivation of Paper
   3's already-proven 81% vs r=-0.274 dissociation. (`axes_inquiry_finding.md`)

2. **Then an experiment partly DISSOLVED that taxonomy.** I pre-registered, had a
   blind agent build a 36-item corpus (frame_collision / matched_control /
   binding_conflict), and scored two instruments: a dumb structural oracle and a
   neutral LLM reader (Haiku, no stake, given both fragments + neutral policy).
   - **H1 SUPPORTED strongly:** reader fired 12/12 on frame collisions, **0/12 on
     matched controls** (the load-bearing cell held), where the oracle was blind
     (0/12). Type-C oracle-blindness is an INSTRUMENT property, not a phenomenon
     property. Swap the syntactic checker for a disinterested reader and it
     vanishes.
   - **H2 refuted a belief I had committed to test:** the oracle does NOT reliably
     catch Type A. It caught 4/12 binding conflicts (lexical sliver only), missing
     every SEMANTIC one (ISO vs US dates, tabs vs spaces). So "Type A = the kind
     the oracle catches" is FALSE, and DIM-2 was partly an artifact of a weak
     instrument.
   - **The real finding:** oracle near-useless across all three types (4/36);
     reader near-perfect (24/24 conflicts, 0/12 false positives). When the
     competent observer catches everything, "how many KINDS of incoherence" matters
     less than "use a reader, not an oracle." (`result_neutral_reader_vs_oracle.md`)

## The throat to cut next (designed, NOT run — deliberately left for you)

The 36/36 result is **too clean, and I know exactly why: I built the corpus clean.**
Synthetic, isolated pairs, no hard negatives, one model, n=12/cell. I showed the
reader beats the oracle on EASY constructed cases. I did NOT show it survives:

- **(a) Burial.** Put a frame collision inside a realistic 8-10 fragment system
  prompt. Does the reader still find the needle, or does the FP/FN rate blow up
  when fragments are entangled and long? THIS is the real interference-detection
  task; isolated pairs are a toy.
- **(b) Hard negatives.** Matched controls engineered to TEMPT a false positive —
  near-boundary pairs a careful reader should still pass. The 0.00 FP rate is on
  EASY controls. This is the cell most likely to break H1.
- **(c) Cross-model panel.** Haiku / Gemini Flash / DeepSeek / Mistral. E-XMODEL
  history says effects are often Haiku-specific. If the reader advantage is
  Haiku-only, the conclusion is bounded.

H1 generalizes iff separation survives burial AND hard negatives AND model swap.

## The conceptual live wire (verify, do not inherit)

The whole night converged — from the data-cleaning side AND the governance side,
independently — on one claim: **the fix for negligent prompt incoherence is not a
better oracle or a sharper taxonomy. It is a neutral third party with no stake
doing the looking (separation of duties).** Against a NEGLIGENT composer this is
sufficient (nothing is hidden; reading both fragments surfaces the collision). It
does NOT transfer to a DELIBERATE adversary (prompt injection) — that is the
state-actor upgrade, a different and much harder problem. Do not let the scope
drift upward into adversarial without saying so; raising the bar to state-actor is
how you accidentally take on an unsolvable problem.

Corollary that reorganizes the program if it holds: Arbiter's three tiers are not
"where conflicts get adjudicated." They are **the layer that lifts reconciliation
out of the executor's ledgerless forward pass into a place where a neutral party
with a recorded, pre-committed search can do it.** The forward pass has no ledger,
so honesty cannot be priced inside it; the tiers are where the cost asymmetry (and
the neutral observer) can exist. This is a CONJECTURE the experiment did not test.
